\begin{abstract}
Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) learn abstract representations by predicting masked regions in latent feature space rather than reconstructing raw pixel or voxel intensities. This design raises a fundamental question for medical imaging: \emph{can an architecture trained without voxel-level reconstruction perform dense, per-voxel multi-compartment volumetric segmentation?} We address this through a controlled benchmark on the BraTS 2024 Adult Glioma dataset evaluating Whole Tumor (WT), Tumor Core (TC), and Enhancing Tumor (ET) segmentation. We assess a teacher-free single-encoder \textbf{3D VisReg JEPA} with multi-scale FPN and UNETR hybrid decoders against matched from-scratch Vision Transformer controls and established 3D convolutional baselines (Residual UNet and deeply supervised 3D nnU-Net). On held-out test volumes ($N=242$), 3D VisReg JEPA demonstrates that latent prediction successfully supports dense segmentation, reaching $76.58\%$ mean Dice ($86.13\%$ WT, $65.99\%$ ET) with a multi-scale FPN and $75.61\%$ mean Dice ($87.19\%$ WT) with a hybrid UNETR decoder. Furthermore, completely freezing the pre-trained ViT backbone achieves $72.31\%$ mean Dice ($84.79\%$ WT) with a pure latent multi-scale FPN probe and $75.13\%$ ($85.72\%$ WT) with a hybrid UNETR probe, demonstrating that self-supervised latent prediction learns rich, spatially decodable tumor representations prior to downstream adaptation. However, controlled comparisons establish three critical boundary conditions: (1) under full supervision ($100\%$ labels), JEPA pre-training matches from-scratch ViT training ($75.15\%$ and $74.80\%$), while 3D nnU-Net maintains the performance ceiling ($79.50\%$) due to localized convolutional inductive biases; (2) under severe annotation scarcity ($1\%$ labels, $12$ volumes) and moderate scarcity ($5\%$ labels, $58$ volumes), pre-trained VisReg UNETR establishes top overall performance across the entire benchmark ($63.65\%$ mean Dice at $5\%$), outperforming from-scratch ViT ($56.57\%$, $+7.08\,\text{pp}$) and deeply supervised 3D nnU-Net ($59.86\%$, $+3.79\,\text{pp}$), while maintaining the lead at $10\%$ data ($66.16\%$ vs.\ $65.03\%$ for nnU-Net); and (3) early joint patch projection renders Vision Transformers vulnerable to missing-sequence triage ($6\text{--}16\%$ mean Dice on single sequences), whereas CNNs preserve strong channel redundancy ($82.06\%$ WT on FLAIR-only for nnU-Net). These findings establish that while 3D JEPA provides superior label efficiency under annotation scarcity, clinical deployment requires channel-decoupled tokenization.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Automated volumetric segmentation of pathological brain lesions in multi-parametric Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI)---such as gliomas imaged via co-registered T1, post-contrast T1 (T1c), T2, and Fluid-Attenuated Inversion Recovery (FLAIR) acquisitions---is a cornerstone of neuro-oncology diagnosis, surgical boundary planning, and conformal radiotherapy targeting~\citep{brats2024,menze2014multimodal}. Fully supervised 3D convolutional architectures, notably 3D U-Net~\citep{ronneberger2015u,cciccek20163d}, V-Net~\citep{milletari2016vnet}, and 3D nnU-Net~\citep{isensee2021nnunet}, have long established the performance standard in volumetric medical image segmentation. Their success is commonly attributed to high-resolution isotropic spatial feature hierarchies and dense horizontal skip connections, which route low-level edge, gradient, and texture cues directly from early encoder layers into the volumetric decoder, ensuring precise spatial localization of anatomical boundaries.

However, training supervised 3D deep models from random initialization requires hundreds of densely contoured 3D volumetric masks delineated across dozens of contiguous axial slices by expert neuroradiologists. Manual voxel contouring is prohibitively expensive, labor-intensive, and subject to marked inter-observer discordance~\citep{menze2014multimodal}. Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) offers an appealing paradigm to alleviate annotation bottlenecks by pre-training representation backbones on unannotated imaging cohorts prior to downstream supervised adaptation.

Generative self-supervised approaches, such as 3D Masked Autoencoders (3D MAE)~\citep{he2022masked,feichtenhofer2022masked}, learn representations by masking spatial voxels or patches and training an expansive decoder to reconstruct raw voxel intensities in image space. In multi-modal brain MRI, however, high-frequency voxel variance is heavily contaminated by non-biological acquisition artifacts: radiofrequency thermal noise (characterized by non-central Rician distributions~\citep{gudbjartsson1995rician}), scanner-dependent $B_1$ magnetic field inhomogeneities, and microstructural tissue speckle. Devoting model capacity to 3D voxel-level reconstruction forces the network to allocate expressive resources to uninformative acquisition noise rather than underlying anatomical morphology and pathological lesion boundaries.

\paragraph{The 3D JEPA Paradigm and the Voxel Paradox}
To overcome the limitations of pixel and voxel reconstruction, Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA)~\citep{assran2023ijepa,lecun2022path} operate strictly within latent feature space. Instead of reconstructing masked voxels, an online 3D context encoder processes visible volumetric tokens, and a lightweight predictor network is trained to forecast the latent representations of masked 3D target blocks. By omitting a pixel-level decoder entirely, JEPA is designed to learn abstract semantic representations and spatial contextual relationships while discarding high-frequency noise.

This foundational design choice leads directly to a central research question and an apparent scientific paradox:
\begin{quote}
\centering
\textbf{\emph{Can 3D architectures designed explicitly to avoid voxel-level reconstruction perform dense, per-voxel volumetric segmentation across multiple anatomical tumor compartments?}}
\end{quote}
At first glance, the objectives appear fundamentally incompatible. Dense volumetric segmentation requires voxel-accurate delineation of infiltrative tumor boundaries across 3D space, whereas JEPA's objective explicitly incentivizes the encoder to discard high-frequency spatial variation in favor of abstract semantic representations. If low-level spatial details are discarded during pre-training, can the learned representations support downstream boundary delineation across distinct tumor sub-compartments?

\newpage
\paragraph{Scope and Contributions}
In this work, we investigate this question through a controlled empirical study comparing 3D Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures against standard supervised 3D convolutional baselines in multi-modal volumetric brain MRI segmentation under the clinical BraTS 2024 GLI three-region protocol: Whole Tumor (WT), Tumor Core (TC), and Enhancing Tumor (ET). Rather than relying on early dual-encoder JEPA variants that require heuristic momentum teachers, we evaluate \textbf{3D VisReg JEPA}~\citep{wu2026visreg}, a single-encoder formulation that prevents latent representation collapse through decoupled optimal transport feature regularization. To systematically probe the architectural bottleneck of Vision Transformers in dense volumetric prediction, we evaluate two decoder paradigms: a hierarchical multi-scale 3D Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) and a convolutional skip-connection hybrid (UNETR Hybrid)~\citep{hatamizadeh2022unetr}, paired with matched from-scratch ViT controls and frozen encoder representation probes.

Our contributions are fivefold:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{First Multi-Region Benchmark for 3D Latent JEPA}: We evaluate single-encoder 3D VisReg JEPA across the community-standard BraTS 2024 GLI three-region protocol (Whole Tumor, Tumor Core, Enhancing Tumor) on held-out test volumes ($N=242$). We confirm the clinical difficulty hierarchy ($\text{Dice}(\text{WT}) > \text{Dice}(\text{TC}) > \text{Dice}(\text{ET})$) across all architectures and quantify the performance gap relative to deeply supervised 3D CNNs ($88.38\%$ vs.\ $87.19\%$ WT; $82.20\%$ vs.\ $77.79\%$ TC; $67.92\%$ vs.\ $65.99\%$ ET).
    \item \textbf{High-Performing Frozen Encoder Representation Probing}: We evaluate frozen backbone representation quality prior to any fine-tuning. Completely freezing the 3D ViT backbone achieves $72.31\%$ mean Dice with a pure latent multi-scale FPN probe (decoding strictly from transformer embeddings without raw-image skips) and $75.13\%$ with a hybrid UNETR probe ($85.72\%$ WT, $77.09\%$ TC, $62.59\%$ ET), proving that latent masked pre-training natively discovers localized spatial representations that transfer to multi-compartment segmentation without gradient backpropagation through the encoder.
    \item \textbf{Significant Label-Efficiency Gains in Data Scarcity}: Under moderate data scarcity ($5\%$ labels, $58$ patient scans), pre-trained VisReg UNETR achieves the top performance across the entire benchmark ($63.65\%$ mean Dice), outperforming matched from-scratch ViT ($56.57\%$, $+7.08\text{pp}$) and deeply supervised 3D nnU-Net ($59.86\%$, $+3.79\text{pp}$). For the FPN decoder, pre-training provides an even larger $+10.58\text{pp}$ gain ($61.45\%$ vs.\ $50.87\%$). VisReg UNETR maintains this lead at $10\%$ labels ($66.16\%$ vs.\ $65.03\%$ for nnU-Net).
    \item \textbf{Controlled Pre-Training vs.\ From-Scratch Equivalence on Full Data}: By evaluating strictly matched from-scratch ViT controls, we show that under abundant annotations ($100\%$ labels), VisReg JEPA pre-training and from-scratch initialization converge to similar representations ($76.58\%$ vs.\ $75.15\%$ for FPN; $75.61\%$ vs.\ $74.80\%$ for UNETR), while convolutional inductive biases in 3D nnU-Net preserve the overall performance ceiling ($79.50\%$).
    \item \textbf{Empirical Boundary Conditions: Modality Fragility vs.\ CNN Redundancy}: We identify a fundamental divergence in representation robustness: while all architectures tolerate additive Rician noise and $B_1$ bias fields ($<1.1\text{pp}$ drop), Vision Transformers experience severe degradation when individual pulse sequences are omitted ($6\text{--}16\%$ mean Dice on FLAIR-only, $5\text{--}13\%$ on T1c-only), whereas convolutional baselines preserve significant cross-channel redundancy ($82.06\%$ WT Dice on FLAIR-only for nnU-Net).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background: Progression of the JEPA Family}
\label{sec:background}

Self-supervised non-contrastive learning in latent space faces a fundamental degenerate failure mode: \emph{latent representation collapse}, where the encoder maps all input patches to a constant vector, driving prediction loss to zero without learning informative representations~\citep{bardes2021vicreg,zbontar2021barlow}. The evolution of the JEPA family reflects progressive advances in preventing this collapse.

\paragraph{Dual-Encoder Architectures with EMA Teachers}
The foundational Image-JEPA (I-JEPA) framework~\citep{assran2023ijepa} prevents representation collapse by maintaining two separate parameter sets: an online context encoder $E_\theta$ updated via gradient descent, and a target teacher encoder $E_{\bar{\theta}}$ updated via an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of online weights:
\begin{equation}
    \bar{\theta}_t \leftarrow m \bar{\theta}_{t-1} + (1 - m) \theta_t, \qquad m \in [0.996, 1.0]
\end{equation}
Combined with stop-gradient operators on target representations, the slow evolution of the teacher prevents instantaneous representational collapse~\citep{grill2020bootstrap}. However, in 3D volumetric medical imaging, the dual-encoder EMA framework introduces acute drawbacks:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{GPU Memory Footprint}: Storing an identical copy of the 3D vision backbone doubles parameter memory during pre-training, severely restricting 3D subvolume batch sizes on memory-constrained GPUs.
    \item \textbf{Heuristic Scheduling}: Training stability depends heavily on empirical cosine scheduling of teacher momentum $m_t = m_{\text{end}} - (m_{\text{end}} - m_{\text{start}}) \cdot \frac{1}{2}(1 + \cos(\pi t / T_{\text{anneal}}))$ ($m \in [0.996, 1.0]$); improper schedules can induce representation destabilization or asymptotic collapse.
    \item \textbf{Implicit Mechanism}: Rather than optimizing an explicit objective that penalizes low representation rank or collapsed feature geometry, non-collapse under EMA updating is an emergent optimization phenomenon.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{LeJEPA (SIGReg): Teacher-Free Sketched Gaussian Regularization}
To eliminate the EMA teacher, LeJEPA~\citep{balestriero2025lejepa} introduced Sketched Isotropic Gaussian Regularization (\textbf{SIGReg}), enabling single-encoder JEPA training. By the Cram\'er-Wold theorem~\citep{cramer1936theorems}, a multivariate distribution on $\mathbb{R}^D$ is uniquely determined by the family of its 1D marginal projections onto the unit hypersphere $\mathbb{S}^{D-1}$. SIGReg samples random 1D projections and penalizes the discrepancy between the empirical characteristic function (ECF) of projected features and the standard normal characteristic function via the Epps--Pulley test statistic~\citep{epps1983test}. While SIGReg removes the momentum teacher, it couples representation location, coordinate scale, and higher-order distribution shape into a single non-parametric test statistic, which can complicate optimization dynamics when 3D features require simultaneous scaling and de-correlation.

\paragraph{3D VisReg: Decoupled Optimal Transport Regularization}
Variance-Invariance-Sketching Regularization (\textbf{VisReg})~\citep{wu2026visreg} refines teacher-free statistical regularization by formally decoupling three geometric degrees of freedom in representation space: (1) batch centering, (2) coordinate scale regularization, and (3) distributional shape regularization via 1D Sliced-Wasserstein Distance (SWD)~\citep{bonneel2015sliced,kolouri2019generalized,villani2009optimal}. Importantly, while foundational single-encoder non-contrastive methods such as LeJEPA~\citep{balestriero2025lejepa} and 2D VisReg~\citep{wu2026visreg} were originally formulated under multi-view invariance paradigms ($\|\mathbf{z}_1 - \mathbf{z}_2\|^2$ across augmented global views), our work synthesizes the spatial masked target prediction objective of I-JEPA~\citep{assran2023ijepa} with VisReg's decoupled geometric non-collapse regularization in full 3D volumetric space, formulating the first single-encoder 3D Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture for dense medical image segmentation.

\paragraph{Rationale for Focusing on 3D VisReg JEPA}
In this study, we investigate 3D VisReg JEPA as the representative member of the JEPA family for four concrete reasons:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Teacher-Free Single-Encoder Simplicity}: It eliminates the memory overhead and momentum hyperparameter tuning of dual-encoder momentum architectures (e.g., EMA teachers), making full 3D volumetric pre-training practical on standard accelerators.
    \item \textbf{Principled Geometric Decoupling}: By separating coordinate variance anchoring from non-Gaussian shape penalties, VisReg prevents opposing gradient dynamics between variance expansion and distribution matching.
    \item \textbf{Decoupled Representation Space}: By applying non-collapse regularization to an auxiliary lower-dimensional projector space ($d_p = 128$), the primary encoder feature manifold ($D = 384$) remains unconstrained, allowing it to represent complex anatomical patterns without being forced into an isotropic distribution.
    \item \textbf{Preservation of Low-Rank and Long-Tailed Data Manifolds}: As established by~\citet{wu2026visreg}, real-world visual and medical data naturally exhibits an intrinsic low-rank covariance structure with a long-tailed eigenvalue decay. Rigid isotropic Gaussian regularizers (such as SIGReg in LeJEPA~\citep{balestriero2025lejepa}) or strict identity-covariance targets (such as VICReg~\citep{bardes2021vicreg}) penalize anisotropic spectral decay by forcing uniform variance across all coordinate axes, suppressing the natural manifold geometry and artificially amplifying uninformative high-frequency noise along minor directions. By decoupling coordinate scale anchoring from higher-order Sliced-Wasserstein shape matching, VisReg preserves the intrinsic low-rank, long-tailed spectral geometry of multi-parametric brain MRI while guaranteeing strict protection against representation collapse.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Multi-Modal 3D Volumetric Processing and Preprocessing}
\label{sec:data_processing}

Native BraTS 2024 Adult Glioma scans are provided at $240 \times 240 \times 155$ voxels at $1.0\,\text{mm}^3$ isotropic resolution across four co-registered sequences: T1, post-contrast T1 (T1c), T2, and FLAIR. To focus computational resources on intracranial tissue and standardize inputs, we extract the 3D bounding box containing all non-zero brain voxels and resample the cropped volume to a canonical grid of $H = W = D = 128$ voxels using trilinear interpolation for images and nearest-neighbor interpolation for ground-truth masks. Let $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 4 \times 128 \times 128 \times 128}$ denote a mini-batch of normalized multi-parametric volumes: $\mathbf{X} = [\mathbf{X}_{\text{T1}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{T1c}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{T2}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{FLAIR}}]$.

The downstream segmentation task follows the official BraTS 2024 GLI post-treatment multi-compartment specification~\citep{brats2024} with 5-class integer voxel annotations: $0:\text{background}$, $1:\text{non-enhancing tumor core (NETC)}$, $2:\text{surrounding non-enhancing FLAIR hyperintensity (SNFH)}$, $3:\text{enhancing tissue (ET)}$, and $4:\text{resection cavity (RC)}$. Following official BraTS 2024 standards, three clinically actionable, overlapping anatomical sub-regions are derived at inference via union over class predictions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Whole Tumor (WT)}: $\mathbf{Y}_{\text{WT}} = \mathbf{Y}_{\text{NETC}} \cup \mathbf{Y}_{\text{SNFH}} \cup \mathbf{Y}_{\text{ET}} = \{1, 2, 3\}$ (total infiltrative tumor extent, best visualized on FLAIR).
    \item \textbf{Tumor Core (TC)}: $\mathbf{Y}_{\text{TC}} = \mathbf{Y}_{\text{NETC}} \cup \mathbf{Y}_{\text{ET}} = \{1, 3\}$ (resectable tumor bulk without peritumoral edema).
    \item \textbf{Enhancing Tumor (ET)}: $\mathbf{Y}_{\text{ET}} = \{3\}$ (actively vascularized, contrast-enhancing rim, best delineated on T1c).
\end{itemize}
The post-surgical resection cavity ($\text{RC} = \{4\}$) represents the fluid-filled surgical void, which is segmented as foreground channel 4 during training to prevent false-positive tumor predictions, but is appropriately excluded from active tumor metrics (WT, TC, ET) following the official challenge protocol.

\paragraph{Volume-Level Non-Zero Voxel Standardization}
To avoid slice-wise intensity inconsistencies and preserve 3D gradient continuity, each volume is independently Z-score standardized across its non-zero intracranial parenchyma~\citep{menze2014multimodal,bakas2018identifying,isensee2021nnunet}:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\mathbf{X}}_{c}(v) = \frac{\mathbf{X}_c(v) - \mu_{c, p}}{\sigma_{c, p} + \epsilon}, \qquad \forall v \in \Omega_{\text{fg}} = \{u \in \mathbb{R}^3 \mid \mathbf{X}_c(u) > 0\}
\end{equation}
where $\mu_{c, p}$ and $\sigma_{c, p}$ are the empirical mean and standard deviation of channel $c$ within the intracranial brain mask $\Omega_{\text{fg}}$ of patient volume $p$, and $\epsilon = 10^{-8}$. Voxels outside the brain mask are set to zero.

\paragraph{Random Modality Dropout (RMD)}
In clinical neuro-oncology, complete four-sequence protocols are frequently compromised by patient motion, acquisition time limits, or contraindications to gadolinium contrast. To instill intrinsic robustness to missing sequences, volumes undergo Random Modality Dropout during training: each sequence channel is independently zeroed out with probability $p_{\text{drop}} = 0.25$~\citep{havaei2016hemis}. A deterministic fallback ensures that if all four channels are selected for dropout, one channel is randomly preserved, preventing empty inputs while maintaining reproducibility.

\paragraph{Patient-Level Stratified Partitioning}
To prevent data contamination, dataset partitioning is enforced at the patient volume directory level ($\mathcal{P}_{\text{train}} \cap \mathcal{P}_{\text{val}} = \emptyset$ and $\mathcal{P}_{\text{train}} \cap \mathcal{P}_{\text{test}} = \emptyset$ across $1,621$ valid processed BraTS GLI volumes)~\citep{brats2024}. Cases are grouped by base patient identifier to guarantee zero longitudinal scan leakage across splits and stratified by tumor volume quartile and enhancing tumor presence using patient-grouped stratification~\citep{pedregosa2011scikit} ($70.6\%$ train, $14.5\%$ val, $14.9\%$ test; $1,144$ train, $235$ validation, and $242$ held-out test volumes), ensuring that lesion volume distributions and enhancing tumor proportions remain consistent across splits. Fractional low-data subsets ($1\%$ to $50\%$) inherit this quartile stratification.

\subsection{3D VisReg JEPA Formulation}
\label{sec:visreg_formulation}
Extending the 2D natural-image formulation of VisReg~\citep{wu2026visreg} and 3D spatiotemporal masked predictive architectures~\citep{assran2023ijepa,bardes2024vjepa,hatamizadeh2022unetr} to dense volumetric medical imaging, we formulate 3D VisReg JEPA as follows:

\paragraph{3D Vision Transformer Backbone}
The encoder $E_\theta$ partitions a 3D volume into non-overlapping $8 \times 8 \times 8$ volumetric patches, yielding a spatial grid of $N_{\text{vox}} = (128 / 8)^3 = 16 \times 16 \times 16 = 4,096$ patch tokens. A 3D convolutional patch embedding layer projects each $8^3 \times 4$ cuboid to embedding dimension $D = 384$. Each token corresponds to an isotropic spatial span of $8 \times 8 \times 8$ voxels in the canonical grid. To incorporate 3D spatial geometry, separable 3D sinusoidal coordinate positional embeddings are added:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{E}_{\text{pos}}^{3\text{D}}(x, y, z) = \left[ \mathbf{e}_{\text{sin/cos}}(x) \,;\, \mathbf{e}_{\text{sin/cos}}(y) \,;\, \mathbf{e}_{\text{sin/cos}}(z) \right] \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times 4096 \times 384}
\end{equation}
The token sequence passes through $L = 8$ Pre-Layer Normalization Transformer blocks with residual skip connections, $h = 6$ attention heads (projection dimension $d_k = 64$), and MLP expansion ratio $4$ ($D_{\text{ff}} = 1536$) with GELU activations.

\paragraph{Stochastic 3D Patch Masking and Predictor}
During pre-training, the 3D patch grid is partitioned into context and target sets:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Anisotropic Target Aspect-Ratio Jitter}: Rather than isotropic cubic blocks, we sample $K = 6$ anisotropic target cuboids with varying 3D aspect ratios scaled to the $16^3$ patch grid: $\mathcal{S} = \big[(6,6,6),\, (4,6,6),\, (6,4,6),\, (6,6,4),\, (4,4,4),\, (4,4,4)\big]$, yielding $216 + 144 + 144 + 144 + 64 + 64 = 776$ target patches ($18.9\%$ of the 4,096-patch grid). These multi-aspect slots capture directional, non-axis-aligned glioma infiltrative morphology across axial, coronal, and sagittal planes while maintaining identical physical millimeter volume coverage. Target blocks are accepted only if intracranial tissue occupancy satisfies $\text{tissue\_count} \ge \max(2, \lfloor 0.25 \times \text{vol} \rfloor)$, preventing rejection deadlock while eliminating background-air prediction shortcuts.
    \item \textbf{3D Connected Context Cluster}: Unmasked candidate patches are assembled using a 3D 26-connected Breadth-First Search (BFS) starting from an intracranial tissue anchor to form a spatially contiguous 3D context cluster $\mathcal{I}_{\text{ctx}}$ of $N_{\text{ctx}} = 1536$ patches ($37.5\%$ of the volume). Evaluating strictly visible context tokens reduces self-attention compute by $85.9\%$ ($1536^2 / 4096^2$) compared to dense masking.
\end{enumerate}
Only context patches $\mathcal{I}_{\text{ctx}}$ pass through the online encoder $E_\theta$, producing context representations $\mathbf{Z}_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times N_{\text{ctx}} \times 384}$. Target representations $Y_{\text{tgt}} = E_\theta(\mathbf{X})[:, \mathcal{I}_{\text{tgt}}, :]$ are generated by forwarding the full volume through the same online encoder under \texttt{torch.no\_grad()}.

A narrow 4-block Transformer predictor $P_\phi$ ($D_{\text{pred}} = 192$, $h = 6$) receives the projected context tokens concatenated with learnable target mask token queries $\mathbf{e}_{\text{mask}} + \mathbf{P}_{\text{tgt}}$, predicting the target representations:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{Y}_{\text{tgt}}^{(k)} = \text{Linear}_{192 \to 384}\left(\text{LN}\left(P_\phi\left(\left[(\text{Linear}(\mathbf{Z}_{\text{ctx}}) + \mathbf{P}_{\text{ctx}}) \,;\, (\mathbf{e}_{\text{mask}} + \mathbf{P}_{\text{tgt}}^{(k)})\right]\right)[:, N_{\text{ctx}}:, :]\right)\right)
\end{equation}
The prediction loss is computed via Smooth $L_1$ (Huber) loss against asymmetrically Layer-Normalized target representations:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{pred}}(\theta, \phi) = \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=1}^K \text{Smooth}_{L_1}\left(\hat{Y}_{\text{tgt}}^{(k)}, \, \text{LayerNorm}(Y_{\text{tgt}}^{(k)})\right)
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Decoupled Center-Scale-Shape Regularization}
To isolate non-collapse regularization from spatial representations, tissue-filtered context tokens are mapped through a 2-layer Projector MLP: $g_\psi: \mathbb{R}^{384} \to \mathbb{R}^{1024} \to \mathbb{R}^{d_p}$ ($d_p = 128$). The projected tissue tokens $\mathbf{Z} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times d_p}$ undergo decoupled optimal transport regularization:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Center Regularization ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{center}}$)}: Mean feature vectors $\boldsymbol{\mu}_z = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{j=1}^T \mathbf{z}_j$ are penalized to enforce zero-centered representations:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{L}_{\text{center}}(\mathbf{Z}) = \frac{1}{d_p} \|\boldsymbol{\mu}_z\|_2^2
    \end{equation}
    Centered features are then defined as $\mathbf{z}_{c, i} = \mathbf{z}_i - \boldsymbol{\mu}_z$.
    \item \textbf{Coordinate Scale Regularization ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{scale}}$)}: Channel standard deviations $\sigma_d = \sqrt{\frac{1}{T}\sum_i z_{c, i, d}^2 + \epsilon}$ are anchored to unit variance via a two-sided squared penalty:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{L}_{\text{scale}}(\mathbf{Z}) = \frac{1}{d_p} \sum_{d=1}^{d_p} (1.0 - \sigma_d)^2
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Feature Standardization}: Tokens are standardized with stop-gradient on both mean and scale (center is handled only by $\mathcal{L}_{\text{center}}$):
    \begin{equation}
        \mathbf{z}_{s, i, d} = \frac{\mathbf{z}_i - \text{sg}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_z)}{\text{sg}(\sigma_d) + \epsilon}
    \end{equation}
    ensuring $\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{z}_s] = \mathbf{0}$ and $\text{diag}(\text{Cov}(\mathbf{z}_s)) = \mathbf{I}_{d_p}$, completely isolating shape from scale.
    \item \textbf{Distributional Shape Regularization ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{shape}}$)}: $M = 256$ random 1D unit vectors $\mathbf{u}_m \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I}_{d_p}) / \|\mathbf{u}_m\|_2$ are sampled. Standardized tokens are projected ($y_{i, m} = \mathbf{z}_{s, i}^T \mathbf{u}_m$) and sorted into order statistics $y_{(1), m} \le \dots \le y_{(T), m}$. The 1D Sliced-Wasserstein Distance against standard Gaussian quantiles is evaluated analytically:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{L}_{\text{shape}}(\mathbf{Z}) = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{m=1}^M \frac{1}{T} \sum_{i=1}^T \left| y_{(i), m} - \sqrt{2}\,\text{erf}^{-1}\left(2\left(\frac{i - 0.5}{T}\right) - 1\right) \right|^2
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
The total 3D VisReg pre-training objective is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{VisReg}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{pred}} + \lambda_{\text{reg}}(t)\left(\lambda_{\text{center}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{center}} + \lambda_{\text{scale}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{scale}} + \lambda_{\text{shape}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{shape}}\right)
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_{\text{center}} = \lambda_{\text{scale}} = \lambda_{\text{shape}} = 1.0$ and $\lambda_{\text{reg}}(t) \in [0.10, 1.0]$ is the cosine regularizer annealing schedule. Following pre-training, the Projector MLP $g_\psi$ is discarded, and the ViT backbone is transferred to downstream volumetric segmentation.

\subsection{Bridging Latent Space to Voxel Space: Decoder Topologies}
\label{sec:decoder}

To systematically investigate how spatial representations are recovered from $8^3$ patch tokens ($16^3 = 4,096$ grid), we evaluate two distinct decoder architectures:

\paragraph{1. Multi-Scale 3D FPN Decoder}
Tokens from four uniformly spaced ViT blocks:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{Z}_{\text{multi}} = \left\{ \mathbf{Z}^{(L_2)}, \, \mathbf{Z}^{(L_4)}, \, \mathbf{Z}^{(L_6)}, \, \mathbf{Z}^{(L_8)} \right\}, \qquad \mathbf{Z}^{(l)} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 4096 \times 384}
\end{equation}
are reshaped into $16 \times 16 \times 16$ spatial grids and progressively upsampled across channel tiers $(192, 96, 48, 24)$ via 3D transposed convolutions and lateral multi-scale fusion units at scales $16^3 \to 32^3 \to 64^3 \to 128^3$. 3D Group Normalization~\citep{wu2018group} is applied throughout. This pure Transformer-decoder architecture relies entirely on latent feature interpolation without external convolutional skip paths.

\paragraph{2. 3D UNETR Hybrid Decoder}
Following \citet{hatamizadeh2022unetr}, the UNETR Hybrid architecture augments the 3D ViT backbone with an expansive convolutional residual decoder equipped with horizontal skip connections. The multi-parametric input volume $\mathbf{X}$ is projected through consecutive $3 \times 3 \times 3$ convolutional residual blocks at native resolution $128^3$ and half resolution $64^3$. Intermediate Transformer representations from blocks $L_2, L_4, L_6, L_8$ are projected and reshaped into volumetric grids, then fused via deconvolution and residual convolution units with the early-stage convolutional skip features. This hybrid design introduces local convolutional inductive biases into the decoding pathway, directly testing whether high-resolution skip routing overcomes the patch granularity barrier.

\subsection{Controlled Experimental Arms and Baselines}
\label{sec:baselines}

To establish rigorous scientific attribution, we formulate a comparative matrix evaluating eight experimental configurations across four architectural paradigms (along with frozen encoder probes):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{3D nnU-Net Baseline (DynUNet with Deep Supervision)}: The self-configuring DynUNet architecture from MONAI~\citep{isensee2021nnunet} with residual blocks and 4 multi-scale supervision heads ($128^3, 64^3, 32^3, 16^3$), totaling $22.75\text{ M}$ parameters. Deep supervision weights $[0.533, 0.267, 0.133, 0.067]$ prevent gradient attenuation.
    \item \textbf{3D Residual UNet Baseline (MONAI ResUNet)}: A 5-stage symmetric convolutional encoder-decoder with Instance Normalization, LeakyReLU ($0.01$), and concatenation skips ($19.23\text{ M}$ parameters)~\citep{ronneberger2015u,cciccek20163d}.
    \item \textbf{3D ViT-UNETR Hybrid (From Scratch)}: The UNETR hybrid architecture ($29.52\text{ M}$ parameters) trained from random weight initialization under the identical supervised schedule.
    \item \textbf{3D VisReg JEPA (UNETR Hybrid)}: Identical UNETR hybrid architecture where the ViT backbone is initialized from the 200-epoch VisReg JEPA self-supervised checkpoint.
    \item \textbf{3D ViT-FPN (From Scratch)}: The pure ViT-FPN architecture ($29.43\text{ M}$ parameters) trained from random initialization.
    \item \textbf{3D VisReg JEPA (Multiscale FPN)}: Identical ViT-FPN architecture initialized from the 200-epoch VisReg JEPA checkpoint.
\end{enumerate}
All models operate within a comparable parameter envelope ($19\text{--}30\text{ M}$ parameters) and train on identical data splits.

\subsection{Downstream Loss Objective and Multi-Region Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:loss_and_metrics}

Models output 5 logits channels corresponding to $\{0:\text{bg}, 1:\text{NETC}, 2:\text{SNFH}, 3:\text{ET}, 4:\text{RC}\}$ and are trained by optimizing a combined multi-class Soft Dice and Cross-Entropy objective:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{Dice}}(\hat{\mathbf{Y}}, \mathbf{Y}) + \mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}(\hat{\mathbf{Y}}, \mathbf{Y})
\end{equation}
where multi-class Soft Dice computes overlap exclusively across tumor sub-compartments ($\text{include\_background}=\text{False}$ over channels $c \in \{1,2,3,4\}$) with squared predictions:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Dice}} = 1.0 - \frac{1}{4}\sum_{c=1}^4 \frac{2 \sum_i \hat{P}_{c, i} \mathbf{Y}_{c, i} + \epsilon}{\sum_i \hat{P}_{c, i}^2 + \sum_i \mathbf{Y}_{c, i}^2 + \epsilon}, \qquad \hat{\mathbf{P}} = \text{softmax}(\hat{\mathbf{Y}})
\end{equation}
Cross-Entropy operates across all 5 classes including background to penalize false-positive foreground predictions on normal brain parenchyma~\citep{isensee2021nnunet}.

\paragraph{Multi-Region Metrics and Inference Protocol}
Following the BraTS community protocol, test predictions are evaluated by first deriving the three overlapping binary region masks: $\hat{\mathbf{M}}_{\text{WT}} = \bigvee_{c \in \{1,2,3\}} \hat{\mathbf{Y}}_c$, $\hat{\mathbf{M}}_{\text{TC}} = \bigvee_{c \in \{1,3\}} \hat{\mathbf{Y}}_c$, and $\hat{\mathbf{M}}_{\text{ET}} = \hat{\mathbf{Y}}_3$. For each region $r \in \{\text{WT}, \text{TC}, \text{ET}\}$, we compute:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Volumetric Dice Score ($\text{Dice}_{r}$)}: $\frac{2 |\hat{\mathbf{M}}_r \cap \mathbf{M}_r|}{|\hat{\mathbf{M}}_r| + |\mathbf{M}_r|}$.
    \item \textbf{Intersection-over-Union ($\text{IoU}_{r}$)}: $\frac{|\hat{\mathbf{M}}_r \cap \mathbf{M}_r|}{|\hat{\mathbf{M}}_r \cup \mathbf{M}_r|}$.
    \item \textbf{95th Percentile Hausdorff Distance ($\text{HD95}_{r}$)}: Symmetric boundary surface distance computed on the canonical $128^3$ grid under unit coordinate spacing (reported in grid voxels to prevent ambiguity with physical patient space, as inputs are standardized via brain bounding box cropping and resampling).
    \item \textbf{Macro-Average Mean Dice}: $\text{Dice}_{\text{mean}} = \frac{1}{3}(\text{Dice}_{\text{WT}} + \text{Dice}_{\text{TC}} + \text{Dice}_{\text{ET}})$.
\end{enumerate}
Inference employs Test-Time Augmentation (TTA) averaging softmax probability predictions across four axial/coronal/sagittal flip orientations. Latency is measured in milliseconds per volume under AMP FP16 on a dedicated GPU with CUDA synchronization. Standard deviations ($\pm$) capture inter-subject anatomical variance across the $N=242$ held-out test volumes for deterministic runs ($\text{seed}=42$).


\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experimental_setup}

\paragraph{Dataset and Cohort Partitions}
Experiments are conducted on the official BraTS 2024 Adult Glioma cohort (\texttt{BraTS GLI})~\citep{brats2024}. The $1,621$ processed $128^3$ volumes are partitioned into $1,144$ training volumes ($70.6\%$), $235$ validation volumes ($14.5\%$), and $242$ held-out test volumes ($14.9\%$) using base-patient grouped stratification over tumor volume quartiles and enhancing tumor presence.

\paragraph{Training Regimes}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Pre-Training}: 3D VisReg JEPA undergoes 200 epochs of self-supervised latent pre-training on the unlabeled training cohort ($B=32$ with AMP FP16, AdamW base $\text{lr}=5 \times 10^{-4}$, weight decay $0.05$). Optimization employs dual synchronized cosine schedules: (i) a 10-epoch linear warmup followed by cosine annealing decay down to $\eta_{\text{min}} = 1 \times 10^{-5}$ across epochs 11--200, and (ii) a 30-epoch regularizer warmup holding $\lambda_{\text{reg}} = 1.0$ followed by cosine annealing relaxation down to $\lambda_{\text{min}} = 0.10$ across epochs 31--200 to prevent representation plateaus.
    \item \textbf{Downstream Supervised Fine-Tuning}: All architectures (VisReg ViT-FPN, DynUNet, Residual UNet) are trained for 60 epochs on the full training split ($1,144$ volumes) with batch size $4$ (under AMP FP16), AdamW ($\text{lr}=3 \times 10^{-4}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$), and Random Modality Dropout ($p_{\text{drop}}=0.25$).
    \item \textbf{Low-Data Regimes}: Models are trained for 25 epochs across six annotation tiers: $1\%$ ($12$ volumes), $5\%$ ($58$ volumes), $10\%$ ($115$ volumes), $25\%$ ($286$ volumes), $50\%$ ($572$ volumes), and $100\%$ ($1,144$ volumes) with $\text{lr}=3 \times 10^{-4}$ and batch size 4 (under AMP FP16). Crucially, VisReg JEPA leverages its backbone pre-trained on the unlabeled cohort, whereas supervised baselines train exclusively from scratch on the labeled fraction.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Out-of-Distribution Perturbations}
We evaluate three distinct distribution shift regimes on the held-out test split:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{3D Rician Noise}: Non-central Rician noise ($\sigma = 0.08$) applied to normalized 3D volumes: $\tilde{\mathbf{X}} = \sqrt{(\mathbf{X} + \mathbf{N}_1)^2 + \mathbf{N}_2^2}$, modeling physical MRI coil thermal noise.
    \item \textbf{Synthetic $B_1$ Magnetic Bias Field}: A smooth quadratic 3D spatial gradient $\mathbf{B}_1(x, y, z) = s \cdot (x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - 0.5)$ with $s = 0.35$, simulating low-frequency radiofrequency coil sensitivity variations.
    \item \textbf{Missing Modality Emergency Triage}: Single-sequence triage protocols evaluating models under missing sequences: T1c-only ($[0, \text{T1c}, 0, 0]$) and FLAIR-only ($[0, 0, 0, \text{FLAIR}]$).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Key Observations}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Hierarchical Regional Segmentation Difficulty}: Across all eight evaluated architectures, test performance strictly mirrors the anatomical volume and clinical contrast hierarchy:
    \begin{equation}
        \text{Dice}(\text{WT}) > \text{Dice}(\text{TC}) > \text{Dice}(\text{ET})
    \end{equation}
    Whole Tumor (incorporating diffuse peritumoral edema) exhibits the highest overlap ($84.8\text{--}88.4\%$ Dice) and lowest surface error ($5.6\text{--}6.7\text{ voxels}$ HD95). Tumor Core exhibits moderate overlap ($72.0\text{--}82.2\%$ Dice), while the third evaluated sub-compartment proves substantially harder ($60.2\text{--}67.9\%$ Dice) with pronounced spatial variance ($\text{HD95} \approx 10\text{--}30\text{ voxels}$). This confirms that smaller, fragmented structures are severely penalized by voxel boundary mismatches.
    \item \textbf{Dominance of Convolutional Inductive Biases}: Deeply supervised 3D nnU-Net establishes the empirical performance ceiling across all three compartments ($88.38\%$ WT, $82.20\%$ TC, $67.92\%$ ET; mean $79.50\%$), followed closely by 3D Residual UNet ($86.45\%$ WT, $79.13\%$ TC, $64.42\%$ ET; mean $76.67\%$). The dense spatial feature hierarchies and localized inductive biases of 3D convolutions consistently surpass self-attention backbones when full training annotations are available.
    \item \textbf{Frozen-Backbone Spatial Decodability (Pure Latent vs.\ Hybrid Probing)}: Completely freezing the 3D ViT backbone (\texttt{freeze\_encoder=True}) yields $72.31\%$ mean Dice ($84.79\%$ WT, $71.97\%$ TC, $60.18\%$ ET) with the pure latent multi-scale FPN probe (which decodes strictly from transformer tokens without raw-image skips) and $75.13\%$ mean Dice ($85.72\%$ WT, $77.09\%$ TC, $62.59\%$ ET) with the hybrid UNETR probe (which combines frozen ViT tokens with trainable high-resolution raw-volume convolutional stems). This shows that 3D VisReg JEPA learns spatially organized multi-compartment representations during self-supervised pre-training that support dense decoding without fine-tuning the transformer weights.
    \item \textbf{Controlled Pre-Training vs.\ From-Scratch Equivalence on Full Data}: Under complete supervised training ($100\%$ labels), VisReg JEPA pre-training achieves modest test-split differences relative to training identical architectures from random weight initialization ($+1.43\text{pp}$ for FPN, $76.58\%$ vs.\ $75.15\%$; $+0.81\text{pp}$ for UNETR, $75.61\%$ vs.\ $74.80\%$). On the validation split ($N=235$) used for checkpoint selection, from-scratch ViT-UNETR reaches $74.52\%$ best Dice vs.\ $74.10\%$ for VisReg UNETR ($-0.42\text{pp}$), while VisReg FPN ($73.45\%$) and Scratch FPN ($73.29\%$) are virtually tied ($+0.16\text{pp}$). This confirms that dense supervised optimization from random initialization readily discovers competitive representations when annotations are abundant, bounding the utility of pre-training to data-scarce regimes.
    \item \textbf{Representation Geometry and Latency}: Across pre-trained, fine-tuned, and from-scratch ViT representations, tissue-filtered Effective Spectral Rank ($S^2$) ranges from $27.51$ ($7.2\%$) to $46.20$ ($12.0\%$) across the $D_{\text{enc}} = 384$ representation manifold (Pretrained Frozen: $\text{erank}=45.08$ [$11.7\%$]; VisReg FPN: $\text{erank}=44.24$ [$11.5\%$]; VisReg UNETR: $\text{erank}=41.37$ [$10.8\%$]; Scratch FPN: $\text{erank}=46.20$ [$12.0\%$]; Scratch UNETR: $\text{erank}=27.51$ [$7.2\%$]), confirming the absence of complete dimensional collapse ($S^2 \to 1$). In inference throughput, standard 3D Residual UNet is fastest ($546.94\,\text{ms}$), followed by 3D nnU-Net ($1066.39\,\text{ms}$) and ViT backbones ($1187\text{--}1374\,\text{ms}$).
\end{enumerate}

The low-data sweep exposes clear regimes in self-supervised representation utility based on annotation volume:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Decisive JEPA Pre-Training Superiority at Moderate Scarcity ($5\%$ and $10\%$ Budgets)}: At $5\%$ annotated patient volumes ($58$ scans), self-supervised pre-training yields its most dramatic empirical advantage. 3D VisReg JEPA paired with the UNETR hybrid decoder surges to $\mathbf{63.65\%}$ mean Dice ($78.14\%$ WT, $64.46\%$ TC, $48.35\%$ ET), outperforming the matched from-scratch ViT-UNETR ($56.57\%$) by an impressive $\mathbf{+7.08\text{pp}}$, while beating deeply supervised 3D nnU-Net ($59.86\%$) by $\mathbf{+3.79\text{pp}}$ and standard 3D Residual UNet ($52.65\%$) by $\mathbf{+11.00\text{pp}}$. At $10\%$ labels ($115$ volumes), VisReg UNETR remains the top-performing model across the entire benchmark ($\mathbf{66.16\%}$ mean Dice), outperforming nnU-Net ($65.03\%$), Scratch UNETR ($61.40\%$, $+4.76\text{pp}$), and ResUNet ($60.46\%$).

At $100\%$ training data, frozen VisReg UNETR reaches $73.85\%$ mean Dice ($85.48\%$ WT, $75.83\%$ TC, $60.25\%$ ET). Moreover, the pure multi-scale FPN probe (which decodes strictly from frozen transformer representations without raw-volume convolutional bypass) achieves $71.38\%$ test Dice ($72.31\%$ with TTA on the full test split), demonstrating that the pre-trained latent space encodes spatially decodable representations across all three tumor sub-compartments without end-to-end backpropagation into the transformer.
    \item \textbf{Ultra-Low Data Behavior ($1\%$ Labels, $12$ Volumes)}: Under extreme data scarcity ($12$ training volumes), a notable architectural bifurcation emerges. The lightweight MultiScale FPN decoder paired with VisReg JEPA pre-training retains $22.45\%$ mean Dice (and $20.74\%$ when frozen), outperforming from-scratch FPN ($16.70\%$) and standard Residual UNet ($5.86\%$). In contrast, high-capacity UNETR hybrid decoders, which incorporate multi-stage convolutional skip projections, struggle to optimize from only 12 volumes ($2.7\text{--}3.2\%$). Deeply supervised 3D nnU-Net maintains $28.05\%$ due to its built-in multi-level auxiliary loss guidance. Crucially, the apparent collapse of standard Residual UNet ($5.86\%$) and UNETR ($2.7\text{--}3.2\%$) reflects an optimization artifact of fixed epoch budgeting: 25 epochs on 12 volumes with batch size 4 yields merely $75$ total gradient updates (compared to $7,150$ updates across 25 epochs at $100\%$ data, $17,160$ updates across 60 epochs, and $7,200$ pre-training steps), which is insufficient for high-capacity convolutional skip projections to traverse the loss landscape. In contrast, pre-trained JEPA features paired with the pure FPN decoder provide an effective initialization scaffold within these few updates.
    \item \textbf{Convergence on Abundant Data ($\ge 25\%$ Labels)}: Beyond $25\%$ labels ($286$ volumes), supervised models begin converging toward full-capacity performance. At $100\%$ labels, deeply supervised nnU-Net reaches $76.31\%$ (without TTA; $79.50\%$ with TTA), closely followed by VisReg UNETR ($75.85\%$) and 3D Residual UNet ($74.74\%$), confirming that self-supervised pre-training primarily benefits constrained annotation regimes.
\end{enumerate}

The missing sequence evaluation reveals a fundamental divergence in representational robustness:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Convolutional Channel Redundancy}: 3D convolutional networks exhibit remarkable cross-channel robustness. Under FLAIR-only input (which provides high contrast for vasogenic edema), 3D nnU-Net maintains an impressive $\mathbf{82.06\%}$ Whole Tumor Dice (only a $5.7\text{pp}$ drop from clean baseline) and $56.25\%$ mean Dice. Similarly, under T1c-only input (which provides sharp contrast on enhancing tumor margins), nnU-Net preserves $\mathbf{64.83\%}$ TC Dice and $\mathbf{51.57\%}$ ET Dice ($60.75\%$ mean Dice). Residual UNet similarly preserves $68.49\%$ WT on FLAIR and $45.31\%$ TC on T1c.
    \item \textbf{Catastrophic Representation Collapse in Vision Transformers}: In stark contrast, Vision Transformer architectures suffer near-total representational failure when sequences are omitted. Pure ViT-FPN collapses to $8.16\text{--}10.91\%$ mean Dice on FLAIR-only ($10.1\text{--}16.9\%$ WT) and $7.52\text{--}7.88\%$ mean Dice on T1c-only. UNETR Hybrid provides minor mitigation on FLAIR-only ($19.74\%$ WT for VisReg and $36.77\%$ when frozen) due to its convolutional skip connections, but collapses on T1c-only ($8.16\text{--}12.88\%$ mean Dice).
    \item \textbf{Why Random Modality Dropout Failed to Regularize Joint Patch Projections}: This catastrophic degradation in 3D Vision Transformers is especially striking because Random Modality Dropout (RMD, $p_{\text{drop}} = 0.25$) was explicitly applied during supervised fine-tuning. Two primary factors explain why fine-tuning RMD proved insufficient to prevent this failure:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \emph{Combinatorial Scarcity of Single-Modality Optimization Steps}: Under independent Bernoulli modality dropping with $p_{\text{drop}} = 0.25$, the probability of dropping three channels simultaneously (leaving a single sequence active) is $p_{\text{single}} = \binom{4}{3}(0.25)^3(0.75)^1 \approx 4.69\%$ across any channel, and only $(0.25)^3(0.75) \approx 1.17\%$ for any specific target sequence like T1c-only or FLAIR-only. Over $98.8\%$ of supervised optimization steps trained the model on multi-modality combinations with at least two active channels. Consequently, gradient descent rarely optimized weights under severe single-channel triage regimes.
        \item \emph{Early Joint Linear Projection Entanglement and LayerNorm Distribution Shift}: More fundamentally, standard 3D ViT / UNETR architectures employ an early joint 3D convolutional patch projection $\mathbf{W}_{\text{patch}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times (4 \times 8^3)}$ that contracts all four sequences simultaneously into the initial token embedding $\mathbf{z}_{0, i} = \sum_{c=1}^4 \mathbf{W}_{\text{patch}, c} \mathbf{x}_{c, i} + \mathbf{b}$. Setting three channels to zero abruptly zeroes out $75\%$ of the input weights' activations at the very first layer. This failure is compounded by the mathematical interaction between training-time inverted dropout and inference-time input scaling: during supervised training, Random Modality Dropout rescaled surviving channels by $C / \text{keep} = 4.0\times$ to preserve expected energy ($\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{x}\|^2]$). When unscaled missing-channel inputs are passed at inference, the surviving modality enters with a $75\%$ energy deficit relative to the fixed sinusoidal coordinate embeddings $\mathbf{E}_{\text{pos}}^{3\text{D}}$, causing static positional cues to dominate biological image features. Because Layer Normalization standardizes activations across the full embedding dimension $D$, this produces massive out-of-distribution shifts in token covariance that trigger cascading attention collapse ($\text{Softmax}(\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^\top / \sqrt{d_k})$) across all 8 transformer layers. In contrast, 3D CNNs apply localized spatial kernels followed by Instance Normalization, which is mathematically scale-invariant: $\text{InstanceNorm}(\alpha \mathbf{X}) = \text{InstanceNorm}(\mathbf{X})$ for any scalar factor $\alpha > 0$. Spatial normalization independently normalizes each channel map across 3D coordinates, cancelling scalar energy deficits and preserving critical spatial activations ($82.06\%$ WT on FLAIR-only for nnU-Net).
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Architectural Mitigation: Independent Per-Channel Tokenization and Attention Aggregation}: Rather than relying exclusively on data augmentation, mitigating this vulnerability requires decoupling the early patch projection topology:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \emph{Independent Per-Channel Patch Embeddings}: Instead of early joint projection, each sequence $c \in \{\text{T1}, \text{T1c}, \text{T2}, \text{FLAIR}\}$ should be processed by an isolated, modality-dedicated 3D patch embedding $\mathbf{W}_c \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times (1 \times 8^3)}$, yielding distinct per-channel token representations $\mathbf{z}_{c, i} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ at patch location $i$.
        \item \emph{Permutation-Invariant Modality Aggregation}: The per-channel token representations can subsequently be aggregated at each spatial location via channel-wise cross-attention, learnable modality gating, or dynamic average pooling over active sequences:
        \begin{equation}
            \mathbf{z}_i = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{M}|} \sum_{c \in \mathcal{M}} \mathbf{z}_{c, i}, \quad \text{where } \mathcal{M} \subseteq \{1, 2, 3, 4\} \text{ is the set of available sequences}.
        \end{equation}
        Under this late-aggregation paradigm, omitting missing sequences simply removes absent channels from the index set $\mathcal{M}$ without perturbing the projection weights or token activation scale of the surviving sequences, ensuring that single-modality triage inputs remain fully on-manifold.
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Mechanistic Representation Probing: Effective Rank and Frozen-Backbone Spatial Decodability}
\label{sec:results_probing}

\begin{figure}[ht]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{figures/latent_geometry_and_probing.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Latent Representation Geometry and Frozen Encoder Representation Probing.} (A) Effective Spectral Rank ($S^2$) across encoders confirming non-collapsed covariance spectra ($D_{\text{enc}}=384$). (B) Downstream segmentation performance of frozen decoder probes vs.\ full fine-tuning. Completely freezing the pre-trained ViT encoder achieves $72.31\%$ mean Dice ($84.79\%$ WT) with pure multi-scale FPN (decoding strictly from latent tokens) and $75.13\%$ with the UNETR hybrid probe (incorporating high-resolution skip convolutions), matching random scratch initialization ($74.80\%$) and confirming out-of-the-box multi-compartment representation discovery without backpropagation through the transformer.}
\label{fig:latent_probing}
\end{figure}

To rigorously assess whether the self-supervised representations learned by 3D VisReg JEPA encode biologically meaningful structures rather than relying on downstream supervised backpropagation, we conduct two mechanistic analyses:

\paragraph{1. Dimensional Geometry \& Effective Spectral Rank ($S^2$)}
Following \citet{roy2007effective} and \citet{wu2026visreg}, we compute the Effective Spectral Rank $S^2 = \exp(-\sum_{i=1}^D p_i \ln p_i)$ of the covariance matrix across tissue-filtered test token embeddings, where $p_i = \sigma_i^2 / \sum_j \sigma_j^2$ are normalized covariance eigenvalues. In complete representation collapse, $S^2 \to 1.0$, while a uniform isotropic sphere yields $S^2 \to D = 384$.
As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:latent_probing}A:
\begin{itemize}
    \item The self-supervised pre-trained 3D VisReg JEPA backbone exhibits an Effective Rank of $S^2 = 45.08$ ($11.7\%$ of maximum dimension $D=384$), while fine-tuned VisReg FPN exhibits $S^2 = 44.24$ ($11.5\%$) and fine-tuned VisReg UNETR exhibits $S^2 = 41.37$ ($10.8\%$).
    \item Supervised from-scratch controls exhibit $S^2 = 46.20$ ($12.0\%$) for ViT-FPN and $S^2 = 27.51$ ($7.2\%$) for ViT-UNETR.
    \item Across all evaluated encoders, representations remain far above complete dimensional collapse ($S^2 \to 1.0$) while preserving the natural long-tailed spectral decay intrinsic to volumetric anatomical images.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{2. Frozen Encoder Representation Probing}
In dense volumetric medical imaging, representation probing evaluates whether pre-trained latent features can be decoded into spatial multi-compartment segmentations without updating backbone weights (\texttt{freeze\_encoder=True}). Because mapping $16^3 = 4,096$ patch tokens back to $128^3$ per-voxel predictions requires multi-stage spatial upsampling, we evaluate two complementary non-linear decoder probing regimes:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Pure Latent Probe (VisReg FPN Frozen)}: Decodes strictly from intermediate ViT token embeddings ($L_2, L_4, L_6, L_8$) without raw-volume convolutional bypass, achieving $72.31\%$ mean Dice ($84.79\%$ WT, $71.97\%$ TC, $60.18\%$ ET). This demonstrates that the frozen pre-trained latent space itself retains localized multi-compartment spatial structure decodable without raw-image skip connections.
    \item \textbf{Frozen Hybrid Probe (VisReg UNETR Frozen)}: Combines the frozen latent representations with trainable high-resolution raw-volume convolutional residual stems (\texttt{stem\_128} and \texttt{stem\_64}), reaching $75.13\%$ mean Dice ($85.72\%$ WT, $77.09\%$ TC, $62.59\%$ ET)---within $0.5\text{pp}$ of full end-to-end fine-tuning ($75.61\%$).
    \item \textbf{Scientific Interpretation}: Together, these probing results address the central research question posed in Section~\ref{sec:introduction}: 3D latent JEPA pre-training preserves localized multi-compartment tumor representations that can be spatially decoded into clinical sub-regions without backpropagating gradients through the 3D Vision Transformer backbone.
\end{itemize}

\section{Discussion, Practical Guidance, and Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Structural Analysis: Why Can 3D JEPA Segment, and Where Does It Fall Short?}
\label{sec:resolving_paradox}

Our empirical findings clarify the central question posed in Section~\ref{sec:introduction}: can an architecture optimized to avoid voxel reconstruction perform dense volumetric segmentation across multiple anatomical tumor compartments? The results demonstrate that 3D JEPA pre-training learns representations capable of dense multi-region prediction ($86.13\%$ WT, $77.62\%$ TC, $65.99\%$ ET for FPN; $87.19\%$ WT, $77.79\%$ TC, $61.84\%$ ET for UNETR), while establishing four core architectural lessons:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Latent Topological Continuity and Multi-Compartment Representation}: Rather than discarding spatial structure, predicting masked 3D target cuboids in latent feature space requires the Vision Transformer to model volumetric continuity across adjacent patches. By extracting intermediate representations from across the network ($\mathbf{Z}^{(L_2)}, \mathbf{Z}^{(L_4)}, \mathbf{Z}^{(L_6)}, \mathbf{Z}^{(L_8)}$), downstream decoders retrieve localized spatial coordinate cues, enabling dense per-voxel mask prediction across Whole Tumor, Tumor Core, and Enhancing Tumor sub-compartments.
    \item \textbf{Out-of-the-Box Frozen-Backbone Spatial Decodability}: Completely freezing the pre-trained ViT backbone achieves $72.31\%$ mean Dice ($84.79\%$ WT, $71.97\%$ TC, $60.18\%$ ET) with the pure latent FPN probe and $75.13\%$ mean Dice ($85.72\%$ WT, $77.09\%$ TC, $62.59\%$ ET) with the hybrid UNETR probe. The fact that pure latent decoding without raw-image skip connections achieves $72.31\%$ and hybrid probing reaches within $0.5\text{pp}$ of full end-to-end fine-tuning ($75.61\%$) demonstrates that 3D VisReg JEPA preserves spatially decodable tumor structures during self-supervised pre-training.
    \item \textbf{Pre-Training Utility Boundary: Annotation Scarcity vs.\ Full Supervision}: By including matched from-scratch ViT controls, we establish a clear boundary for self-supervised pre-training utility:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Under complete supervision ($100\%$ labels), VisReg JEPA pre-training and random initialization converge to comparable performance ($76.58\%$ vs.\ $75.15\%$ for FPN and $75.61\%$ vs.\ $74.80\%$ for UNETR on test; $73.45\%$ vs.\ $73.29\%$ for FPN and $74.10\%$ vs.\ $74.52\%$ for UNETR on validation). Dense supervised gradients from over a thousand volumes readily guide random weights to competitive representations.
        \item Under moderate label scarcity ($5\%$ labels, $58$ volumes), self-supervised pre-training provides decisive structural advantages: VisReg UNETR surges to $\mathbf{63.65\%}$ mean Dice ($78.14\%$ WT), outperforming matched from-scratch ViT ($56.57\%$) by $\mathbf{+7.08\text{pp}}$ and deeply supervised 3D nnU-Net ($59.86\%$) by $\mathbf{+3.79\text{pp}}$. At $10\%$ labels ($115$ volumes), it maintains the benchmark lead ($\mathbf{66.16\%}$ vs.\ $65.03\%$ for nnU-Net).
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Inter-Channel Projection Entanglement and Modality Fragility}: In standard 3D Vision Transformers, all four MRI sequences are jointly projected into patch tokens via an early convolutional layer. When individual sequences are omitted, the token representations suffer severe LayerNorm distribution shifts (compounded when training-time $4\times$ inverted-dropout rescaling is omitted at inference) that cascade through multi-head self-attention, causing severe degradation ($6\text{--}16\%$ mean Dice). Supervised fine-tuning with Random Modality Dropout ($p_{\text{drop}} = 0.25$) fails to prevent this drop due to the scarcity of single-modality training steps and the linear entanglement of early weights. Architectural solutions requiring independent per-channel 3D patch embeddings aggregated via late attention pooling or gating are necessary to confer genuine channel-invariance. In contrast, 3D CNNs employ localized filters with additive channel accumulation and Instance Normalization, preserving substantial single-channel spatial activations ($82.06\%$ WT on FLAIR-only for nnU-Net).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Practical Model Selection Guidance}
\label{sec:practical_guidance}

Based on our empirical results, we outline concrete practical considerations for model selection in multi-parametric 3D medical imaging:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Consider 3D VisReg JEPA when}:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \emph{Annotations are scarce ($\le 10\%$ annotated volumes)}: In data-constrained regimes ($58\text{--}115$ patient scans), pre-training 3D VisReg JEPA on available unannotated cohorts and fine-tuning with a UNETR hybrid decoder provides the strongest performance ($63.65\text{--}66.16\%$ mean Dice), outperforming both from-scratch ViTs ($+7.08\text{pp}$) and deeply supervised CNNs ($+3.79\text{pp}$).
        \item \emph{Rapid frozen encoder deployment is desired}: Practitioners seeking to minimize fine-tuning compute or preserve a fixed generalist feature extractor can freeze the pre-trained ViT encoder and train only decoder heads, achieving $72.31\%$ (pure FPN) to $75.13\%$ (hybrid UNETR) mean Dice without backpropagating through the transformer backbone.
    \end{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Prefer Supervised Convolutional Baselines when}:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \emph{Abundant annotations exist ($\ge 25\%$ labels)}: With extensive annotated cohorts, deeply supervised 3D nnU-Net establishes superior accuracy across all anatomical compartments ($88.38\%$ WT, $82.20\%$ TC, $67.92\%$ ET; mean $79.50\%$) and sharper boundary delineation ($5.63\text{ voxels}$ WT, $4.64\text{ voxels}$ TC).
        \item \emph{Emergency triage with missing sequences is anticipated}: Convolutional architectures with localized feature channels provide vastly superior resilience when contrast-enhanced T1c ($64.83\%$ TC for nnU-Net vs.\ $1.84\text{--}4.72\%$ for fine-tuned VisReg ViTs) or FLAIR sequences ($82.06\%$ WT for nnU-Net vs.\ $16.87\text{--}19.74\%$ for fine-tuned VisReg ViTs) are unavailable.
        \item \emph{Inference latency is critical}: Standard 3D Residual UNet is over $2.4\times$ faster than UNETR Hybrid ($546.94\,\text{ms}$ vs.\ $1331.60\,\text{ms}$ per $128^3$ volume).
    \end{enumerate}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Limitations and Methodological Validity}
\label{sec:limitations}

We explicitly delineate seven methodological limitations of this study:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Generative Pre-Training Comparisons}: While this study includes strictly matched from-scratch Vision Transformer controls to isolate architectural vs.\ pre-training effects, we do not evaluate a 3D Masked Autoencoder (3D MAE) baseline under compute-matched schedules to directly contrast latent prediction against voxel-level reconstruction. Similarly, we evaluate VisReg as a representative single-encoder JEPA without an isolated fine-tuning comparison against SIGReg.
    \item \textbf{Nature of Low-Data Background Collapse}: Standard 3D Residual UNet ($5.86\%$ mean Dice) and UNETR Hybrid ($2.7\text{--}3.2\%$ mean Dice) collapse to near-background predictions at $1\%$ labels under the fixed 25-epoch budget ($75$ gradient updates at $B=4$). This reflects an optimization and undertraining failure mode under severe class imbalance and short schedules rather than an intrinsic ceiling of the architectures, as deeply supervised DynUNet achieves $28.05\%$ mean Dice under the identical budget. Furthermore, low-data sweeps evaluate fixed 25-epoch convergence without a validation checkpoint selection loop.
    \item \textbf{Spatial Resolution, Canonical Coordinates, and Post-Treatment Evaluation}: Volumes are resampled from native $240 \times 240 \times 155$ ($1.0\,\text{mm}^3$) to a canonical $128 \times 128 \times 128$ grid following brain bounding box extraction. This introduces case-dependent spatial scaling (typically $\approx 1.2\text{--}1.5\,\text{mm}$ per voxel across subjects), which is why reported HD95 surface distances are explicitly labeled in canonical grid voxels rather than physical millimeters. Regional evaluation strictly follows the official BraTS 2024 post-treatment GLI mapping ($\text{WT}=\{1,2,3\}$, $\text{TC}=\{1,3\}$, $\text{ET}=\{3\}$), where label 4 denotes the surgical resection cavity (RC) and is correctly excluded from active tumor sub-compartments across all compared models.
    \item \textbf{Statistical Scope and Seed Variance}: All benchmark metrics reflect a single deterministic training run ($\text{seed}=42$). The reported standard deviations ($\pm$) capture inter-subject anatomical variance across the $N=242$ held-out test volumes, not variance across random initializations. Multi-seed evaluations with paired bootstrap confidence intervals are needed for formal statistical hypothesis testing.
    \item \textbf{Baseline Training Budgets}: The 3D nnU-Net baseline implements the MONAI DynUNet architecture trained for a matched budget of 60 epochs. Full standalone nnU-Net pipelines train for 1000 epochs with extensive automated data augmentation and ensembling, representing a substantially higher compute regime.
    \item \textbf{Decoupled Projector Regularization and Uncalibrated Regularizer Weights}: In our single-encoder formulation, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{VisReg}}$ regularizes the auxiliary projector branch $g_\psi(\mathbf{Z}_{\text{ctx}}) \in \mathbb{R}^{128}$ while $\mathcal{L}_{\text{pred}}$ operates in the $384$-dimensional encoder space with default weights ($\lambda_{\text{c}}=\lambda_{\text{sc}}=\lambda_{\text{sh}}=1.0$). Because the projector can partially satisfy Gaussian moment matching using positional coordinate variance (with validation Effective Rank dipping from $57.67$ at Epoch 1 to $12.37$ at Epoch 10 before recovering to $41.36\text{--}45.08$ by Epoch 200), we deliberately do not rank methods by Effective Rank: rank is reported only as a binary non-collapse check, and all comparative conclusions rest on downstream segmentation (Dice/HD95).
    \item \textbf{Early Joint Patch Projection Bottleneck}: The 3D ViT tokenization employs a single joint linear projection across all four multi-modal sequences. While standard in UNETR designs, this topology entangles input channels and induces acute sensitivity to omitted modalities during triage. Future architectures should evaluate decoupled, per-channel patch projections paired with permutation-invariant late attention pooling or gating.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this paper, we investigated whether 3D Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA)---designed explicitly to avoid voxel-level reconstruction---can perform dense multi-compartment volumetric medical image segmentation. Through a systematic empirical evaluation on the BraTS 2024 GLI benchmark under the three-region protocol (WT, TC, ET) comparing 3D VisReg JEPA against matched from-scratch ViT controls and supervised convolutional baselines (Residual UNet and deeply supervised DynUNet), we find that 3D JEPA successfully learns representations capable of dense multi-region segmentation, achieving $86.13 \pm 8.51\%$ WT, $77.62 \pm 19.01\%$ TC, and $65.99 \pm 31.07\%$ ET Dice (mean $76.58\%$) with MultiScale FPN and $87.19 \pm 10.65\%$ WT, $77.79 \pm 20.94\%$ TC, and $61.84 \pm 34.70\%$ ET Dice (mean $75.61\%$) when paired with a UNETR hybrid decoder. Furthermore, completely freezing the pre-trained encoder achieves $72.31\%$ mean Dice with the pure latent FPN probe and $75.13\%$ with the hybrid UNETR probe, showing that latent prediction preserves spatially decodable anatomical structures.

However, our empirical benchmark establishes critical architectural lessons and boundary conditions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Fully supervised CNNs remain superior across all compartments on full data ($88.38\%$ WT, $82.20\%$ TC, $67.92\%$ ET for nnU-Net; mean $79.50\%$), demonstrating that localized 3D convolutional priors outperform self-attention for volumetric dense prediction when annotations are abundant.
    \item Self-supervised pre-training provides decisive label-efficiency advantages in constrained annotation regimes ($5\text{--}10\%$ labels), where VisReg UNETR establishes top overall performance across the entire benchmark ($63.65\%$ at $5\%$, outperforming scratch ViT by $+7.08\text{pp}$ and nnU-Net by $+3.79\text{pp}$; and $66.16\%$ at $10\%$).
    \item Vision Transformers exhibit acute representation fragility under missing pulse sequences ($6\text{--}16\%$ mean Dice), whereas convolutional networks preserve substantial spatial redundancy ($82.06\%$ WT on FLAIR-only).
\end{enumerate}
We conclude that 3D JEPA provides a powerful representation foundation for volumetric medical imaging under annotation scarcity, but clinical deployment requires channel-invariant tokenization to match the triage robustness of specialized convolutional networks.
